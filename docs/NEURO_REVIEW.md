# Neuro review: why people stop, try, finish and tell others

An honest read of what a person sees and feels, with the principle behind each choice and what was changed (2026-10-31). No hashtags, no emoji, no tricks that work against the person: the aim is to remove fear and effort, and to make the good moment memorable.

## The journey and its emotions

| Moment | What the person feels | Principle | Verdict and change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sees a post, a video or the README title | Annoyed by a slow, nosy TV; curious if it can be fixed | Curiosity gap; loss aversion (the TV is *already* costing them) | Old title "A calmer TV" described us, not them. New: *Your slow TV, fixed in two minutes* (outcome first, specific number) |
| First 3 seconds of a video | Pattern interrupt or scroll | Hook before payoff | Videos open on the stutter itself, with the line *This stick was dying* (VIDEO_KIT.md) |
| Landing page above the fold | "Is this safe? Is it for me?" | Fewer elements = less cognitive load; Von Restorff (one isolated call to action) | One amber button on a dark page; the objection (*nothing deleted, undo any time*) sits right under the headline. Was a white button among other white controls |
| Choosing a download | Fear of choosing wrong | Choice paralysis (Hick's law) | The page picks the right file for their computer, shows the others small |
| Opening the app the first time (an unknown developer warning) | Doubt | Authority and transparency | README explains the warning, signed files, checksums, open source |
| "Allow debugging?" on the TV | Fear of the unknown | Reduce uncertainty, explain what will happen | The app shows what the dialog is and why; one thing at a time |
| The clean-up running | Anxiety, wanting control | Progress and control | Groups of four, a health check after each, *Stop* and *Undo* always visible |
| **The result screen** | Relief, a small win | **Peak-end rule; variable reward; anticipation** | The free-memory number now counts up from before to after (1.3 s, easing out), then a green *+25 MB* chip pops in; respects reduced motion. Before: a static number |
| Opening Lume for the first time | Delight | Aesthetic-usability effect; micro-animation; warm light | Big glass clock, painting, spring focus; the amber orb is the single warm point of the brand |
| Pairing the phone | Surprise | Instant feedback | The "Phone connected" capsule drops in with a spring and a green dot |
| Telling a friend | Pride, identity ("I fixed my own TV") | Social currency | *Tell the result* copies a sentence with the real numbers; the README's last line invites a star and a TV report |

## Colour (neurocolour)

Dark blue-black background: calm, focus, low glare in a living room. **Amber** (#ff9f0a) as the only warm accent: warmth, energy, attention and the action colour, used for the primary button, the orb and the selected state; green only for success (iOS convention: safe, done); red only for "off" and danger. Blue is kept for links. Contrast of dark text on amber is above 10:1.

## Language

Short Anglo-Saxon words, concrete nouns, one number (*two minutes*, *69 to 81 percent*), the objection answered in the same breath (*nothing deleted*), rule of three where it helps (*find, clean, undo*). The final line of a page or video carries the calm promise before the address: peak-end.

## Dopamine, honestly

The reward has to be real or it is manipulation. Ours: a measurable gain (memory, smoothness), an animation that lets the person *see* it, a way to share it, and a list that grows when they report their TV. We do not use fake scarcity, fake counters, streaks or nagging.
